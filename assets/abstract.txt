Visual text editing aims to precisely modify text in images or videos while preserving stylistic consistency and visual realism. 
Despite significant advances in image text editing, video text editing remains largely unexplored, facing challenges in cross-frame accuracy, temporal coherence, and stylistic fidelity. 
In this paper, we introduce STEER, a unified framework that \textbf{steers} a frozen video diffusion model to perform precise video text editing through stylized glyph control. 
STEER employs a frozen dual-branch diffusion transformer with a lightweight text context adapter that decomposes the editing signal into two orthogonal streams: a style encoder capturing the original text's visual attributes, and a dual-granularity glyph encoder encoding the target text content. 
To overcome the weak text rendering capability of video foundation models, we propose an ocr-guided region reweighting loss and a three-stage progressive training strategy.
To support large-scale training, we also develop a fully automatic data synthesis pipeline and construct STEER-1M, a dataset of one million triples spanning diverse scenes, fonts, and stylistic effects.
Both quantitative and qualitative experiments show that STEER achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming both open-source baselines and the commercial model Seedance 2.0.
